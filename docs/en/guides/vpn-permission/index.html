Why the app asks for VPN permission, and what it grants

The first time you connect, the system asks for permission — a dialog on the phone, an admin prompt on the desktop. Here is what you are actually granting.

Phone and TV

Android lets no app touch another app's traffic on its own. To route it through a tunnel, an app has to register with the system as a VPN — and the system shows a "Connection request" dialog naming the app.

You are asked once. While the permission holds, a key icon stays in the status bar — that is the system reminding you a tunnel is up. You can revoke it in Android settings, in the VPN section.

Desktop: two different routes

  • System proxy — the app opens a local endpoint and writes it into Windows or macOS settings. Everything that reads system settings goes through it: browsers, stores, most apps. No administrator rights needed.
  • Tunnel — traffic goes through a network interface the app creates, so everything goes through it, including software that knows nothing about proxies. Creating that interface is a system-level operation, which is why administrator rights are asked once.

What the permission does not do

VPN permission covers network packets and nothing else. Files, contacts, camera, microphone and other apps' contents stay closed — the system has separate permissions for those, and the app does not request them.

On Android the app also asks for notification permission. That is for exactly one thing: the stop button in the notification shade, so you can drop the tunnel without opening the app.
