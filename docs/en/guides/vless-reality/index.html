VLESS and Reality in plain words

Two words you meet in every modern key. Here is what each one does — enough to understand what you were handed and why it is configured this way.

VLESS is transport with nothing extra

VLESS carries your data and deliberately does nothing beyond that: no encryption of its own, no heavy headers. Encryption is handled by the TLS layer above it.

The practical result is lower overhead — a lighter, faster connection, which shows most on weak hardware such as TV boxes.

Reality is a way to look like an ordinary site

A normal secure endpoint presents a certificate of its own, and that certificate makes it easy to recognise. Reality takes another path: while establishing the connection it borrows the handshake of a real third-party site that you name in the settings.

From the outside it looks like an ordinary visit to that site. Such a node has no certificate at all — so nothing expires, nothing needs renewing, and there is no distinguishing mark to match against.

What the fields in a key mean

  • sni — the name of the site whose handshake is borrowed.
  • pbk — the public half of the server key; the client uses it to confirm it is talking to the right node.
  • sid — a short identifier separating clients on one node.
  • fp — which browser your client imitates. On iPhone it is always chrome; with safari the connection drops.
  • flow — the transfer mode; in our keys it is what lets data pass without an extra wrapping layer.

Why this is not just another protocol

The difference is not speed, it is how the connection looks from outside. Older protocols were recognisable by a characteristic pattern — a problem anywhere the network is picky about unfamiliar traffic.

Reality does not invent a new pattern; it borrows one that is already ordinary. That is the whole idea.
